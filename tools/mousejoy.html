MouseJoy: Two Thumbs, One Cursor

This page is intentionally long. Put the simulated cursor over this document, hold RB, and move the right stick to scroll. While RB is held, the left stick still moves the cursor.

The central idea

Conventional controller-to-mouse software assigns one analog stick to the pointer and leaves the user trapped between two bad choices: a cursor fast enough to cross the screen, or a cursor slow enough to land accurately. MouseJoy treats both sticks as contributors to the same movement vector. Push them together and they reinforce one another. Push them against one another and they brake. Use either stick alone and it remains a perfectly ordinary analog pointer.

The result is not merely a faster cursor. It is a cursor with two simultaneous layers of intention. One thumb can establish the broad direction while the other trims the approach. You can cross a monitor with both sticks, then oppose them as you arrive, without switching modes or reaching for a sensitivity button.

Analog speed control

The triggers modify the combined motion. The left trigger accelerates; the right trigger slows the cursor for precise placement. Their effects are continuous and symmetrical, so overlapping trigger pressure does not create a broken state. Equal pressure cancels cleanly.

Try holding both sticks toward an icon while gently squeezing the left trigger. Then release it and squeeze the right trigger as the cursor approaches. The demonstration preserves fractional movement internally, so very slow motion should still accumulate smoothly instead of vanishing into integer rounding.

Temporary scrolling

Scrolling is too important to bury behind a settings screen. Hold the right shoulder button and the right stick becomes a two-axis scroll wheel. Push down to move down the page, up to move upward, and use horizontal motion where the hovered panel supports it. The left stick remains a cursor throughout.

Releasing the shoulder button restores cooperative dual-stick cursor control. A recenter gate prevents a tilted right stick from throwing the pointer the instant scrolling ends. Return the right stick to center and it quietly rejoins cursor movement.

Persistent scrolling

For long documents, hold RB and click the right stick. MouseJoy enters persistent scrolling mode. You may release RB; the right stick continues controlling scrolling while the left stick controls the pointer. Centering the right stick pauses scrolling without leaving the mode. Hold RB and click the right stick again to return to dual-stick cursor control.

Clicking and dragging

X is the left mouse button, A is the right mouse button, and Y is the middle mouse button. These are real press and release states inside the demonstration, not repeated click commands. Hold X over a title bar and move the sticks to drag a window. Release X to let it go. Double-tap X over a desktop icon to open or close its window.

Why a browser prototype?

A browser cannot move the operating system cursor, but it can reproduce the complete interaction model within this page. That makes this demo a tuning laboratory: movement curves, dead zones, trigger scaling, scrolling, mode transitions, click timing, and visual feedback can all be tested before the native application injects real Windows input.

What comes next

The production MouseJoy rebuild will use the same pure JavaScript/TypeScript control engine inside a native desktop shell. A small Windows-facing layer will read the controller and send relative pointer, wheel, and button events through the operating system. The public web demo remains useful as an interactive explanation of why the design feels different.

Keep scrolling. This last paragraph exists mostly to make the paper long enough to demonstrate gradual scrolling, high-speed scrolling, precise trigger braking, and the difference between a temporary scroll clutch and persistent scrolling. The control scheme should feel understandable before anyone reads a manual: both thumbs move, a shoulder temporarily repurposes, and one deliberate chord makes that repurposing persistent.

About MouseJoy

Not merely “a joystick mouse.”

MouseJoy is a cooperative two-thumb pointer. Both sticks contribute to cursor velocity, the triggers continuously scale speed, and RB turns the right stick into an immediate analog scroll wheel.

Try these gestures

  • Push both sticks together to cross the desktop rapidly.
  • Oppose them slightly to brake near an icon.
  • Hold the right trigger for extremely fine motion.
  • Hold RB and scroll inside this window.
  • Hold X over the title bar and drag the window.
  • Press Start + Back to suspend or resume control.

You may also use the keyboard fallback shown in the help panel when a controller is not available.
